#index 
### Aliases
These are alternative titles for notes that help in searching and linking. They are useful for dual-language notes or for different terms referring to the same concept. They are added in the metadata section of a note. 
### Backward Linking
This involves deliberately shifting attention to the context of an existing note and thinking about how new information can expand on the idea presented there. It requires reading the existing note (or note-sequence) first and then writing a new note that elaborates on the relevance of the new idea. We can then link back to the source of the idea. The opposite is forward linking.
### Canvas
A canvas is like a mind-map where you can arrange notes from your vault. It's like an infinite digital whiteboard where you can brainstorm, diagram, and lay out your ideas. Here you can draw lines, add images, PDF's, Videos and also canvas-specific notes. 
### Fleeting Notes
These are quick, informal notes taken to capture ideas or thoughts as they occur. They are usually written on whatever is at hand and are meant to be processed and turned into permanent notes as soon as possible1
### Forward Linking
This is the practice of adding links to other notes without pausing to check the destination first. It is done to maintain the flow of writing and often points to index-notes or notes that are already on the writer's mind.
### Graph View
This is a visual representation of the interconnectedness of notes in Obsidian. It can show both global and local graphs, helping users navigate through clusters of notes. The graph view is more useful when you apply filters. 
### HUB's 
These are central notes that became the go-to place for navigating different lines of a discussion. Hubs can evolve organically over time and provide structured overviews of topics. In contrast to index-notes they point to different aspects of the same topic, regardless of the terms used. 
### Index Notes
These are notes that together form the index and usually have one-word titles (terms and concepts for one index and names for a separate index). They improve the searchability within the Zettelkasten. In contrast to hubs and MOCs they point to different topics discussed under the same term.
### Metadata
This refers to information about a note, such as the date it was created or last modified. Metadata can be added manually either in the YAML-section on top of a note (everything between two lines of three dashes), with the help of Obsidian's "properties"-feature or inline with a double colon. 
### MOCs
These are higher-order hubs that provide an overview of multiple clusters of notes. They link to other hubs and offer a more abstract level of organization. 
### Note Sequences
These are series of notes that form a train of thought or argument. Each note in the sequence is connected to the previous and following notes, creating a coherent line of reasoning. A single Obsidian note can contain a whole note sequence where each paragraph or block represents a single permanent note. 
### Permanent Notes
This is the general term for all notes in the "Zettel"-folder. Regular permanent notes are carefully phrased notes written to be understood even when the original context is lost. They are meant to be comprehensible in the future and often include references, links to other notes, or are embedded in note-sequences.
